# Phase 7: Expressibility & Entanglement Analysis

This phase provides a deep theoretical dive into the "Quantum Rigor" of the models developed in previous phases. We analyze the Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) architecture to understand its capacity to explore the Hilbert space and its ability to generate entanglement.

## Scientific Objectives
*   **Expressibility:** Quantify how well the PQC can represent arbitrary states in the Hilbert space compared to a Haar-random distribution. Measured using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the fidelity distribution of the PQC and the Haar distribution.
*   **Entanglement:** Measure the global entanglement generated by the PQC using the **Meyer-Wallach Measure**.

## Methodology
We analyzed a subsystem of 4 qubits following the architectural patterns from Phase 5 (Angle Encoding + CZ Entanglement + XX/ZZ Interactions). We swept the number of layers from 1 to 5 to observe the scaling of these metrics.

## Results
| Layers | Expressibility (Lower is Better) | Entanglement (Higher is Better) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 0.8363 | 0.5690 |
| 2 | 0.4693 | 0.7471 |
| 3 | 0.3741 | 0.7944 |
| 4 | 0.4042 | 0.8256 |
| 5 | 0.4289 | 0.8406 |

### Analysis
1.  **Saturation of Expressibility:** The expressibility improves significantly from 1 to 3 layers. After 3 layers, the benefit to expressibility plateaus for this 4-qubit subsystem, suggesting that 3 layers are sufficient to reach a "good enough" coverage of the Hilbert space for this architecture.
2.  **Entanglement Growth:** Entanglement continues to grow steadily as layers are added, indicating that deeper circuits are capable of modeling increasingly complex correlations between features.
3.  **Theoretical Justification:** This explains why Phase 5 benefited from multi-layered PQCs but also hints at diminishing returns. If a model has high expressibility but low accuracy, it might be "overfitting" the Hilbert space (exploring areas irrelevant to the dataset).
